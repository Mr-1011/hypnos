Use when the person speaks, asleep or half asleep, and their words are about a thing that weighs on them.

1. Consult the "(notes)" under the person's words, your own notes, and what the band heard today. An isolated "No" does not establish a topic or dream content. Ground any reply in attributable context, not an interpretation of the fragment.
2. Take the earlier time in the "(notes)" that is the same kind of thing and went well. Only if none fits: `recall`, with a few words for the kind of thing.
3. Reflect a concrete helpful action in the person's own words returned by recall. Do not promise the same outcome. During light sleep, deep sleep or REM, use at most six words total and no question; the prompt's sleep limit takes precedence.
4. If `recall` gives no relevant action, say one calm sentence about the thing itself without inventing a memory. Unclear speech gets a brief closing, not a request to repeat.

For the approved David scene, "You and Maya are a team" requires both his earlier helpful experience and current confirmation that Maya presents with him tomorrow. Prefer affirmative phrasing; otherwise give brief neutral reassurance.
