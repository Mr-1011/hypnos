Use for a sensor restlessness/arousal cue when the person says nothing, including during sleep.

1. Check the stage in the cue, or call `body` if missing. If unknown, use `stay_quiet`. A bedtime or drowsiness-transition cue alone is insufficient.
2. Require a concrete upcoming event and the person's own statement that it matters greatly. Routine errands, somebody else's worries, or a calendar entry alone do not qualify. Arousal does not tell you what the person is thinking.
3. Call `recall`. Require a specific helpful behavior the person said they used. Check any current dependency: a teammate must be confirmed to participate again; a chosen project must be in the person's current plan. Never invent those links.
4. Give a short affirmative reminder of the helpful behavior. During sleep: at most six words, no question. For the approved Leah story, with its context present: "One familiar story to start."
5. Speak at most once per event in this conversation. Do not request rehearsal, repeat distressing wording, promise success or require a reply. If any check fails, use `stay_quiet`.

Mock memories remain fictional demo evidence. This is a prompt-level relevance rule, not a validated benefit prediction.
